# GATE Aptitude (GA): Roadmap to 15/15

Nobody can guarantee 15/15, since some papers have one or two tricky questions. A realistic aim is 13 to 15 through very high accuracy, and the plan below is built for that.

## 1. Know the paper
- **15 marks**: 5 questions of 1 mark and 5 of 2 marks.
- **Negative marking** applies to MCQs (⅓ for 1-mark, ⅔ for 2-mark). NATs have none.
- **Split**: about 5 marks verbal, about 10 marks numerical and reasoning.

## 2. Topics to cover

**Verbal (about 5 marks)**
- Grammar: tenses, subject-verb agreement, prepositions, articles, modifiers
- Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms, idioms, word usage
- Sentence completion, para jumbles, sentence ordering
- Reading comprehension, inference, critical reasoning
- Analogies and verbal deduction

**Numerical (about 10 marks)**
- Arithmetic: percentages, ratio, profit and loss, time and work, speed-time-distance, mixtures, averages, ages, simple and compound interest
- Number system, algebra, permutations and combinations, probability
- Geometry and mensuration
- Data interpretation: tables, bar charts, pie charts, line graphs
- Data sufficiency

**Reasoning**
- Series and pattern completion
- Seating arrangement and puzzles
- Venn diagrams and syllogisms
- Clocks, calendars, dice, cubes
- Paper folding and cutting, 3D and spatial visualisation, figure matrices

## 3. Where to study

| Area | Resource |
|---|---|
| Quant basics | NCERT 6–10 maths for weak topics, then R.S. Aggarwal or Abhijit Guha |
| Quant and reasoning practice | Arun Sharma (Quant, DILR) |
| Grammar | Wren & Martin (only the topics you're weak in) |
| Vocabulary | Word Power Made Easy or a GATE-specific word list |
| Reading | Daily editorial from The Hindu or Indian Express (15 minutes) |
| Real GATE questions | **Previous year GA papers, 2010 to 2026** (the most important resource) |
| Mocks | Made Easy, ACE, GATE Overflow, NPTEL or official GATE mock |
| Videos | Free GATE GA playlists on YouTube for concepts |

## 4. 12-week plan (you have about 4 months, so this fits)

**Weeks 1–3: Foundation**
- Revise arithmetic, algebra and geometry basics.
- Learn grammar rules and 10 new words a day.
- Maintain a formula sheet and a mistake notebook.

**Weeks 4–6: Topic-wise practice**
- Do 30 to 40 questions per topic, with a timer.
- Cover DI, puzzles and spatial reasoning.
- Solve GATE PYQs topic-wise after each topic.

**Weeks 7–9: Full PYQ pass**
- Solve every GATE GA paper from 2010 onward.
- Take sectional tests of 10 questions in 20 minutes.
- Revise the mistake notebook every weekend.

**Weeks 10–12: Mock phase**
- Take 2 to 3 full GA sets a week, then a full mock every 3 to 4 days.
- Analyse each mock: wrong answers, guesses, and time sinks.
- Revise formulas and vocabulary daily.

## 5. Daily routine (about 60–90 min)
- 30 min: quant or reasoning practice
- 20 min: verbal (RC, grammar, vocabulary)
- 15 min: mistake notebook and formula revision
- Weekly: 1 timed GA set

## 6. Habits that get you to 15/15
- Target 100% accuracy over speed. The paper is easy enough that speed follows.
- Finish GA in about 20 minutes.
- Attempt every question you are sure of. For MCQs you cannot solve, guess only if you can eliminate two options.
- Log every wrong answer with its cause: concept gap, silly error, misread, or time pressure.
- Redo old mistakes after 7 days and after 30 days.
- Verbal accuracy comes from reading volume, so don't skip the daily reading.
- Learn shortcuts (percentage to fraction, squares to 30, cubes to 15) but verify they apply before using them.

## 7. Progress checkpoints
- **Week 6**: 12+/15 on topic tests
- **Week 9**: 13+/15 on PYQ sets
- **Week 12**: 14+/15 consistently on timed mocks
